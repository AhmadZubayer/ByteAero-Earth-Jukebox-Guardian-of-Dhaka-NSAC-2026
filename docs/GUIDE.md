# Player guide — Earth Jukebox: Guardian of Dhaka (3D)

Headphones help: sounds come from where they are in the city. Every sound also has a written
description on screen and for screen readers.

## The journey

1. **Title.** Start, or Continue a saved game. Open *Settings and accessibility* first if you need them.
2. **The message.** Echo catches a broken message from an imagined 2050. Type your name for the ticket.
3. **Earth Jukebox.** A globe shows NASA's global temperature from 1884 to 2022. Press *Play the
   time-lapse*: each sitar note is one year, higher and tenser as the world warms. Then *Fly to Dhaka*.
4. **Tutorial (about a minute).** One instruction at a time on a practice street: walk to the yellow
   circle, follow the heat sound, ask Echo to scan, plant a tree, then *Start the game*. You can skip it.
5. **Six years in Dhaka:** 2000, 2010, 2020, 2024 (NASA data), then 2035 and 2050 (imagined).
6. **Earth Record.** Your six years as one piece of music. Play it, or download it as a WAV file.
7. **Ticket.** Print your Earth Listener Ticket.

**Optional: Sound Lab** (button on the title screen). Move Heat, Rain and Green and hear what each
does, see the notes flow in the live visualiser, try the audio legend, then name three clues in Quick Ears.

## Never lost: the mission card and the yellow light

During a round the card at the top left shows your three goals (find the heat, protect the blocks,
the heat wave), the **one next thing to do** ("Rooftop Hill needs 1.1 °C of cooling. Next: plant a
tree"), and which way to go. A **yellow light** marks the spot (it can be turned off in settings as
*Guide light*). If you have not found the heat after 20 seconds, Echo marks it for you, except in 2020,
where the heat stays hidden and you must find it by ear.

## Learning as you play

- **Did you know?** cards pop up when you do something new: find heat (the urban heat island), plant a
  tree (shade and evaporation), paint a roof (reflecting sunlight), dig a pond, open a cooling centre,
  walk into a green block (how NDVI measures plants from space), scan (sonification).
- Each tutorial step teaches one idea too.
- Echo's report lists **what you learned this year** and asks one **quick question**. A right answer
  gives 1 bonus coin for the next year. Answer most of them right for the **Earth Learner** badge.
- Your ticket lists every fact you collected.

## One round

1. **Listen.** Echo warns of a heat wave. A rising sitar line plays from each hot spot.
2. **Find.** Walk toward the sound. It gets louder, higher and faster as you get close, and (with the
   heat view on) glowing rings rise from where it comes from.
   Press **Q** and Echo scans: notes sweep around you; the highest note points to the heat.
   Press **R** and Echo tells you the direction in words.
3. **Protect.** Spend coins on fixes in and around the hot blocks:

   | Key | Fix | Coins | What it does (game estimate) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | Tree (choose mango, coconut palm or neem) | 1 | Shade and cooler air in its block |
   | 2 | White roof | 2 | Stand next to a building; its roof reflects sunlight |
   | 3 | Pond | 3 | Cools its block and a little of the neighbours (needs the block's central square) |
   | 4 | Cooling centre | 4 | People can shelter there. It protects people but does not cool the city |

   Choosing **Tree** opens a picker: mango, coconut palm or neem (all cost the same and cool the same).
   A yellow ring shows where the chosen fix will go. Press **E** to place it.
4. **The heat wave** arrives when the timer ends, or press **F** (or *Bring the wave*). Protected blocks
   stay calm and people cheer; the music settles. Unprotected blocks turn red and the music stays out of tune.
5. **Echo's report** shows the real NASA images and numbers for that year, what they mean, and a piece of
   the message. If more than half the city was left in the heat, try the year again.

**Stars:** 50 % protected = 1 star, 75 % = 2, 90 % = 3.
**Listener bonus:** find a hot spot with the heat view off and without asking Echo for directions: +2 coins.
**Badges:** Heat Hunter (found heat by ear alone), City Cooler (80 % or more on average),
Future Maker (75 % or more in 2050), Earth Learner (quick questions right).

## The six years

| Year | Twist | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| 2000 | One hot spot, plenty of time | Every place has a sound; hot is high |
| 2010 | Two hot spots | Two snapshots are not a trend |
| 2020 | The hot spot is hidden: no colours. Talk to Rina at the tea stall | A satellite sees the ground surface, not how a person feels |
| 2024 | The hottest April air of the four years, and the fewest coins | Budgets force choices; a cooling centre helps people, not the city |
| 2035 (imagined) | Choose clean energy or coal, parks or concrete | Today's choices shape the future |
| 2050 (imagined) | The final wave, shaped by your choices and the trees and ponds you planted | The ending is ours to write |

## Accessibility settings

Sound · Captions · Echo's directions in words · Heat view · Relaxed mode (no timer) · Reduce motion ·
Vibration · Text-only mode · Volume · Graphics quality (Low turns off shadows and glow and halves the crowd).

**Text-only mode** plays every round as a page: a table of the 25 blocks (north at the top), forms to place
fixes, and *Play the city*, which sweeps the blocks as notes from north to south and west to east
(panned left to right; higher notes are hotter). In 2020 you guess the hidden hot spot by ear.

## What the numbers mean

- **Land surface temperature (LST)** is how hot the ground, roofs and roads are when NASA's Terra
  satellite passes (an 8-day daytime average around 15 April). It is not the air temperature and not how
  hot a person feels.
- **NDVI** measures green plants: about 0.1 for bare or built-up ground, 0.6 or more for lush green.
- **NASA POWER** gives the air temperature and rain at one point in Dhaka.
- The streets are invented; each block uses the numbers from the matching part of the NASA image.
- Cooling effects of fixes and the size of each heat wave are game estimates.
- Four April snapshots cannot prove a climate trend. 2035 and 2050 are imagined, not NASA forecasts.
