# Earth Jukebox: Guardian of Dhaka — updated game journey

This is the player journey for the current browser game. It shows what happens from opening the game to getting a ticket. Some story details may change as the game is improved.

## The story in one sentence

Echo, a friendly satellite, receives a broken message from a young person in a possible future Dhaka. The player listens to real pictures of Earth, gathers clues, and helps finish the message.

The message and the people in the story are made up. The NASA images are real and are shown with their dates and sources.

## The whole journey

**Open the game → meet Echo → hear Earth → learn the sounds → travel through six years → choose a future → make a final song → get a ticket**

The game should feel like one unfolding story. Each page should lead naturally to the next. The player should always know what to do: **look, listen, choose, then see what changed**.

### A player's trip, in plain words

“I open the game and meet Echo. A strange message asks me to find out what happened to Dhaka. I scan real pictures of Earth and hear them turn into music. After a short practice, I visit Dhaka in six different years. In each year I find one clue or make one choice. The last two years show a future story shaped by my choices. I put the message back together, listen to the song I made, and get a ticket I can keep.”

## Page 1 — Loading screen

- **Player sees:** Earth slowly comes into view. A small signal travels toward Dhaka. The title appears: *Earth Jukebox: Guardian of Dhaka*.
- **Player hears:** A short, gentle signal. Sound starts only after the player turns it on.
- **Player does:** Waits a moment, then chooses **Start**. A clear **Sound on / Sound off** choice is shown.
- **Next:** The signal opens into a message.

## Page 2 — The message

- **Player sees:** Echo receives a broken message from future Dhaka. Only a few words are clear: “Can you hear what happened to our city?”
- **Player hears:** A short voice line or reads the same words on screen. The message breaks into six pieces, one for each year in the journey.
- **Player does:** Enters a name or uses “Earth Explorer.”
- **Next:** Echo asks the player to learn how pictures can become sound.

**Story note:** The future message is fiction. It should have a visible **Imagined story** label.

## Page 3 — Earth Jukebox

- **Player sees:** Two real NASA Earth Information Center temperature frames, one from 1884 and one from 2022.
- **Player does:** Picks a frame and presses **Scan**. A light travels across the picture from left to right. The player can pause, replay, and switch years.
- **Player hears:** The picture makes sound while the scan moves. Warm and cool colors have different voices. The player can hear the two frames side by side.
- **Player learns:** A sound can help them notice a pattern in a picture. A short label explains what the picture shows and what its colors mean.
- **Next:** Echo opens a small practice room.

**Visual moment:** The scan line becomes a glowing thread. That thread pulls the player from the full Earth view toward Dhaka.

## Page 4 — Sound Lab

- **Player sees:** Three large controls: **Heat**, **Rain**, and **Green space**. A simple picture changes as each control moves.
- **Player does:** Tries each control once. They make a warm scene, a rainy scene, and a calmer scene.
- **Player hears:** Heat changes the melody, rain changes the beat, and green space changes the soft background notes.
- **Player learns:** The controls are practice examples. They are not live measurements.
- **Next:** Echo invites the player to try five short sound clues.

## Page 5 — Quick Ears

- **Player sees:** Five short clues, one at a time. Each clue has sound, words, and a small visual sign.
- **Player does:** Chooses **Heat**, **Rain**, or **Green space**. They can replay a clue. A wrong answer gives a helpful hint.
- **Goal:** Three correct answers complete the practice. The player can try again. A text path lets someone continue even when they cannot hear the sounds.
- **Next:** The Earth picture folds into a map of Dhaka.

## Page 6 — Dhaka journey map

- **Player sees:** A real NASA map of land surface temperature around Dhaka. The date, source, and map key are easy to find. Six year cards sit along the bottom: **2000, 2010, 2020, 2024, 2035, 2050**.
- **Player does:** Opens the first year. Later years unlock as the story moves forward.
- **Player learns:** The first four years use observed images. The last two years are imagined story paths, shown beside a clearly dated real image for context.
- **Next:** The six rounds begin.

### The rule for every round

Each round has four simple steps:

1. **Look** at a dated image or an imagined future scene.
2. **Listen** to its sound, or read the matching sound description.
3. **Choose** one action or answer.
4. **Reveal** a piece of the message and move on.

The story scenes can show a fictional child and family in Dhaka. Their street is an illustration, not a street measured by the NASA map.

## Round 1 — 2000: The first note

- **Player sees:** The real NASA land surface temperature image for April 2000. A small illustrated postcard introduces a fictional family in Dhaka.
- **Player does:** Moves the scan across the image and chooses a marked area to hear again.
- **Player hears:** A short tune made from the selected image area. The area lights up as it plays.
- **Story reveal:** The first piece of the message appears. Echo says, “Every place has a sound. Let’s keep listening.”
- **Next:** The map turns like a page to 2010.

## Round 2 — 2010: Two pictures, one question

- **Player sees:** The real 2000 and 2010 images in a simple side-by-side view. The same map area is marked in both.
- **Player does:** Switches between the two images and plays each sound. Then they pick one thing they noticed.
- **Player hears:** A short call-and-response between the two images.
- **Story reveal:** A second piece of the message appears.
- **Important note:** These are single dated views. The game must not call their difference a long-term climate trend.
- **Next:** Echo moves to 2020.

## Round 3 — 2020: What the picture cannot tell us

- **Player sees:** The real 2020 image. A fictional street scene appears beside it: a walk to school, a bus stop, and people looking for shade.
- **Player does:** Taps the image to hear two marked areas, then chooses which question they would ask a person on the street.
- **Player learns:** A satellite picture can show the land surface. It cannot, by itself, tell us how a person feels or the exact air temperature at a bus stop.
- **Story reveal:** A third piece of the message appears. The fictional person’s answer adds the human side of the story.
- **Next:** The picture slides into 2024.

## Round 4 — 2024: Make a plan

- **Player sees:** The real 2024 image. The player can still return to the earlier images. An illustrated Dhaka street sits in a separate panel marked **Imagined street**.
- **Player does:** Scans the image, then chooses how to help people in the story: add shade, try a reflective roof, or open a cooling center. A short line explains each choice.
- **Player sees next:** The illustrated street changes. The game explains the choice in plain words. For example, a cooling center gives people a safer place during heat; it does not cool the whole city.
- **Story reveal:** The fourth piece of the message appears.
- **Next:** Echo asks, “What kind of future should we imagine?”

## Round 5 — 2035: Choose a path

- **Player sees:** Two clearly marked **Imagined future** scenes. One has more trees and cleaner energy; the other has less shade and more heat-trapping surfaces. The real 2024 image stays available as a dated reference, not as a 2035 map.
- **Player does:** Chooses an energy path and a green-space path. They can preview each scene before deciding.
- **Player hears:** The story music changes with the choice. This music is an illustration of the story, not a NASA forecast.
- **Story reveal:** The fifth piece of the message appears.
- **Next:** The player travels to 2050 to see the ending of their story path.

## Round 6 — 2050: The message is whole

- **Player sees:** The future Dhaka scene shaped by the choices made in 2024 and 2035. Echo puts the six message pieces together.
- **Player does:** Makes one final choice: which lesson should Echo send back to people today? The choices are short and clear, such as **Listen to the data**, **Protect people**, or **Make room for shade**.
- **Player hears:** A short final song built from the Earth sounds they explored. They can replay its parts.
- **Player learns:** This is one possible story ending, not a NASA prediction of 2050.
- **Next:** The final song folds into a ticket.

## Page 7 — The player's Earth song

- **Player sees:** A small music player with the observations they collected. The real image sounds and the imagined story music have different labels.
- **Player does:** Plays the whole song, taps one year to hear it again, or reads a text version of the journey.
- **Next:** The player opens their ticket.

## Page 8 — The ticket

- **Player sees:** A personal **Earth Listener Ticket** with their name, the years they visited, the three things they noticed, their future story choices, and a small image of their song.
- **Player does:** Saves or prints the ticket, or starts again to try a different story path.
- **Ticket wording:** The ticket calls any score a **game score**. It never presents that score as a measured change in Dhaka. It links to the NASA image sources and explains which ending was imagined.
- **Final feeling:** The player leaves with a story they helped make and one clear idea: Earth data can be seen, heard, and explored together.

## Page 9 — Sources and how the sounds work

Players can open this page at any time. It uses short sentences to answer:

- Where did each real image come from?
- What does each image measure?
- How does a picture or data value become a sound?
- Which parts of the game are made up?

The sound should be made while a player scans or changes a real frame. Each sound rule should be shown in words and with a visual sign. A player should be able to complete the whole journey with sound off, by keyboard, and with reduced motion.

## Visual style

- Make the journey feel like a **living picture book from space**: real NASA images form the pages, and illustrated Dhaka scenes rise above them like paper layers.
- Use one repeating visual idea: a glowing thread that starts as a scan line, becomes the path through Dhaka, and ends as the line printed on the ticket.
- Give every year one memorable picture or moment. Avoid making all six rounds look and play the same.
- Use the **Home Video** font for big titles, year cards, and the ticket. Use an easy-to-read font for longer text.
- Keep the real data, the sound rule, and the imagined story clearly labeled.

## What to check with players next

Ask a few young players to try the full journey. Afterward, ask them what was real, what was imagined, and what the sound helped them notice. Use their answers to improve the words, choices, and sound.
